EcoFlow River 2 Bluetooth monitor

There is nothing to wire.

The whole project is one board, and it arrives fully built.

This is the whole project

The yellow screen board, in the cardboard box

A tiny computer, a touchscreen and a Bluetooth radio, all on one board. Its nickname is the Cheap Yellow Display, or CYD. The label on your box calls it ESP-S028R.

Wires to connect: one USB cable. The whole job is to plug the yellow board into your Mac, copy the program onto it, and move it to the van. Eight short steps, about 30 minutes.
1

Open the box

Inside you should find the yellow board and a USB cable. There may also be a small plastic pen for tapping the screen and a short wire with a white plug. Ignore that wire.

Look around the edges of the yellow board for its USB sockets. Every board has a small flat one (micro-USB). Some also have an oval one (USB-C). Either can be used. Step 2 shows which cable goes with which.

Handling it

  • Hold it by the edges, like a photo.
  • Rest it on cardboard, wood or a plain desk. The box it came in is perfect.
  • Never rest it on metal while it is plugged in. That includes the lid or wrist rest of a MacBook. The back of the board has bare metal points, and a metal surface joins them together.
  • Peel off the clear film on the screen if there is one.
2

Plug it into your Mac

The USB cable does two jobs. It powers the board and it carries the program to it. There is no battery, no power supply and no on switch. Plugged in means on.

Use the cable from the box. On the board it goes into the small flat socket (micro-USB). It only fits one way up, so do not force it.

  • If your Mac only has small oval sockets (USB-C), put a USB-C adapter or hub on the Mac end, or use a cable that is USB-C on one end and micro-USB on the other.
  • Two-socket boards: the oval socket works as well, as long as the other end of the cable is a rectangular USB-A plug. A normal phone charging cable is usually this kind.
  • Two-socket boards: an oval-to-oval cable (USB-C on both ends) does not work in the board's oval socket. The board gets no power that way.
You'll know it worked whenthe screen lights up. New boards show a colourful demo. Whatever it shows is fine.
It didn't work
The screen stays black
Push both ends of the cable in firmly. Try a different USB socket on the Mac. Then try a different cable.
The screen is on, but the check at the start of step 5 finds nothing
Many USB cables only carry power and no data. Use the cable from the box, or one you have used to copy files to a phone or camera. Plug it straight into the Mac, not into a dock or hub.
3

Install the tool that talks to the board

You only do this once. Commands are typed into the Terminal app: press ⌘ Space, type Terminal, press Return. For each dark box below, press Copy, paste into Terminal with ⌘ V, press Return, and wait for it to finish.

cd ~/Developer/ecoflow-monitor

Go to the project folder. Do this first every time you open a new Terminal window.

brew install platformio

Installs PlatformIO, the program that copies code onto boards. This takes a few minutes.

pio --version

Checks that it installed.

You'll know it worked whenthe last command prints something like PlatformIO Core, version 6.1.18.

Would you like Claude to type for you?

In Claude, say “Run step 3 of docs/setup-guide.html.” Claude can run every step on this page except step 4, where you type your own password.

It didn't work
command not found: pio
Close the Terminal window, open a new one, run the first command again, then try pio --version again.
4

Give it your EcoFlow user id

Your battery only obeys the EcoFlow account it was set up with in the phone app. The board proves it belongs to you with your account's user id. That is a long number. It is not your email and it is not your password.

python3 tools/get_user_id.py you@example.com

Change you@example.com to the email you log in to the EcoFlow app with. It then asks for your EcoFlow password. Nothing shows while you type. That is normal. Type it and press Return.

Your password goes to EcoFlow's own login server, the same as when you sign in to the app. It is not saved anywhere.

You'll know it worked whenit prints Your user id: 1234567890123456789 with your own number. Select the number and copy it.
cp -n src/secrets.h.example src/secrets.h

Makes your private settings file. It stays on your Mac. If the file already exists, this leaves it untouched.

open -e src/secrets.h

Opens that file in TextEdit.

Find the line that starts with #define ECOFLOW_USER_ID. Paste your number over the example one, then save with ⌘ S and close TextEdit. Leave the other line alone.

It didn't work
Login failed
The email or password is not the one for your EcoFlow account. Check that you can log in to the EcoFlow app with the same two things.
You sign in to the app with Apple or Google and have no password
The script needs an email and password login. Set a password for the account in the EcoFlow app first.
The battery has never been set up in the app
Do that first. Pairing it in the app is what links the battery to your account.
5

Copy the program onto the board

People call this flashing or uploading. First check that the Mac can talk to the board, and is not just powering it. With the board plugged in, run:

ls /dev/cu.*usbserial*

This should print one line, such as /dev/cu.usbserial-1420. That line is the board. If it says no matches found, stop here and open “It didn't work” below. The next command cannot work until this one does.

Then copy the program across:

pio run -e cyd -t upload

The first run takes a few minutes because it downloads what it needs. After that it takes under a minute. Leave the cable alone while it runs.

You'll know it worked whenTerminal ends with a green SUCCESS line, and the board restarts to a black screen with Scanning in the top left corner.
It didn't work
no matches found, or the upload starts with Serial port /dev/cu.Bluetooth-Incoming-Port
The Mac cannot see the board at all, so the tool guessed a port that is not the board. Holding BOOT will not help. It is the cable or the socket. If the board's screen is dark, it has no power: an oval-to-oval cable in the board's oval socket cannot power it. Use the flat micro-USB socket, or a cable with a rectangular USB-A plug on the Mac end. If the screen is lit, it has power but no data: the cable is a charge-only one, so swap it. Either way, plug straight into the Mac instead of a dock or hub, then run the check command again.
The check command printed a line, but the upload is stuck on Connecting........ and then fails
Find the two tiny buttons on the back of the board, labelled BOOT and RST. Press and hold BOOT, run the command again, and let go when Terminal says Writing.
secrets.h: No such file
Step 4 was skipped. Do the two commands that create and open secrets.h.
The upload says SUCCESS but the screen is blank
Unplug the board, wait a second, and plug it back in. If it is still blank, the program is most likely running and only the display setting is wrong. Tell Claude: “The upload worked but the screen is blank.”
The colours look wrong, like a photo negative
Some batches of this board need one extra display setting. Tell Claude: “The colours are inverted.”
The picture is upside down or mirrored
Tell Claude: “The screen is upside down.” It is a one-line change.
6

Let it find the battery

Three things have to be true for the board to connect:

  • The battery is switched on.
  • The EcoFlow app on your phone is fully closed. Swipe it away. The battery talks to one Bluetooth device at a time, so while the app has the connection the board cannot get it.
  • The board is within a few metres of the battery.

Then watch the top left corner of the screen. This is a working copy of what you will see. Tap the labels underneath to see what each message means.

You'll know it worked whenthe dot turns green, the corner says Connected, and the battery percentage appears. This usually takes under 30 seconds.
It didn't work
It stays on Scanning
Nine times out of ten the phone app still has the connection. Swipe the app away, or switch Bluetooth off on the phone for a minute. Also check the battery is on and close by.
It says Auth rejected
The user id in secrets.h is not the one for the account this battery is paired with. Redo step 4 with the right account, then redo step 5.
It keeps cycling between Connecting and Reconnecting
Move the board closer. If it carries on, run pio device monitor in Terminal, copy what it prints, and show it to Claude. Press Ctrl C to stop the monitor.
7

Test the two buttons

Tap AC on the real screen. It shows AC ... for a moment, then turns green and says AC ON once the battery confirms it has switched. Tap again to turn it off. The 12V button works the same way for the car socket. You can practise on the copy of the screen in step 6.

  • Press firmly. This screen senses pressure, unlike a phone. A fingernail or the plastic pen works best.
  • A button that says -- is waiting for the battery to report in. Give it a few seconds.
  • The screen switches itself off after one minute without a touch. Tap anywhere to wake it. That first tap only wakes the screen. It never presses a button, so you cannot switch something off by accident in the dark.
You'll know it worked whenthe button on the screen changes colour and the matching light on the battery itself comes on or goes off.
It didn't work
Nothing happens, or the wrong button reacts
The touch layer needs lining up with the picture. Run pio device monitor, tap each of the four corners of the screen once, and copy the [touch] lines it prints. Give them to Claude and say “Calibrate the touch with these.” Press Ctrl C to stop the monitor.
The button turns red and says RETRY
The battery did not confirm within four seconds. Tap it again.
8

Move it into the van

Unplug the board from the Mac. The program stays on it for good. Then plug the same cable into any USB socket. One of the USB sockets on the EcoFlow itself works well.

As soon as it has power it starts by itself and finds the battery again. No Mac needed.

  • It uses about 1 watt. Left on around the clock, that is roughly 3% of a River 2 Pro per day.
  • If the battery goes out of range or is switched off, the screen says No signal or Scanning. It reconnects by itself when the battery is back.
  • To use the phone app over Bluetooth, unplug the board for a moment. Only one of them can be connected at a time.
  • Do not leave the bare board loose where it can touch metal. Tape it to something or put it in a small plastic case.

Words you will run into

CYD
Cheap Yellow Display. The yellow board. Its formal name is ESP32-2432S028R.
ESP32
The chip on the yellow board that does the thinking. It has Bluetooth built in.
Firmware
The program that runs on the board.
Flash, upload
Copy the firmware from your Mac onto the board.
PlatformIO, pio
The tool on your Mac that builds the firmware and flashes it.
Serial monitor
A live diary the board prints to your Mac over the USB cable. Useful when something is wrong.
BLE
Bluetooth Low Energy. The kind of Bluetooth the battery uses.
secrets.h
Your private settings file. It holds your user id and stays on your Mac.
